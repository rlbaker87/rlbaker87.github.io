Kitchen Hood Fire Safety Training

A mixed-modality safety training program built for external kitchen contractors, focused on fire prevention awareness and emergency response.

4 min Training Video Runtime
3 Fire Response Steps Covered
7 Team Huddle Techniques
1-3 Kirkpatrick Levels Evaluated

The Challenge

Kitchen staff working as external contractors lacked reliable knowledge of how to prevent and respond to kitchen hood fires. Unlike internal team members, these contractors fell outside the organization's standard training accountability structure. High turnover and limited vendor follow-through on training completion meant traditional assignment and tracking methods were not a reliable solution.

Any effective approach needed to work without assuming consistent LMS access or manager oversight — placing immediate, usable information directly in the hands of workers at the point of need.

Solution

  • Designed a color-coded job aid presenting the three fire response steps — fire suppression system activation, gas shut-off, and Class K fire extinguisher use — in a format posted directly at workstations, with a QR code linking to the training video for self-directed access.
  • Wrote, narrated via Azure AI, edited, and produced a 4-minute training video in Camtasia covering fire prevention techniques, equipment location, and a scenario-based walkthrough of the response sequence, including on-site footage captured with a DSLR camera.
  • Developed a one-page team huddle activity guide with seven facilitation techniques for team leads to use during brief, recurring check-ins without pulling staff away from work for extended training blocks.
  • Built a detailed design document specifying scene-by-scene video production notes, infographic layout and color logic, and full narration script for stakeholder review.
  • Structured the program for LMS deployment — searchable and assignable by managers — with the infographic available for download and physical posting at workstations.
  • Mapped evaluation to Kirkpatrick Levels 1 through 3, using LMS survey data, on-site observation, and team huddle simulations to assess engagement, learning, and behavioral transfer.

Job Aid

The color-coded job aid presents all three response steps in a format designed for quick reference at the workstation. A QR code links directly to the training video for on-demand access without requiring LMS login.

Team Huddle Techniques

The program includes seven facilitation techniques designed for team leads to use during brief, recurring check-ins. Each technique is structured for rapid deployment — most run under five minutes — and targets a different aspect of fire safety knowledge and behavior.

What's Wrong Here?

Team inspects a workstation with a deliberate safety issue — such as an incorrectly installed grease filter — and identifies the error in 30 seconds. Targets prevention awareness.

Two-Minute Roleplay

Two or three team members are assigned a live fire scenario and walk through the response steps aloud, assessing both knowledge and communication under pressure.

Scavenger Hunt

Pairs locate the manual pull station, gas shut-off, and fire extinguishers in sequence. Reinforces equipment location through active, on-floor practice.

Quick Quiz

Rapid-fire questions with immediate feedback. Example: a fire develops and spreads — hands up if you know the first response step. Fast assessment, no prep required.

Mini-Demo

Team lead demonstrates one response step, then follows up with a quick quiz on the remaining steps. Effective for targeted remediation of a specific knowledge gap.

Walkthrough

Longer kitchen walkthrough covering prevention checkpoints and equipment locations. Works well at the two-month mark post-training to reinforce retention.

Design Rationale

Team huddle techniques address a core constraint of contractor training — you cannot assume LMS access, scheduled training blocks, or consistent manager follow-through. These techniques are designed to work in under five minutes, on the floor, with no technology required.

Impact & Results

  • Program reached kitchen team members across multiple facilities and was made available through the LMS for both manager assignment and self-directed access via QR code.
  • Evaluation mapped across three Kirkpatrick levels — LMS survey data for Level 1, knowledge checks for Level 2, and team huddle simulations as the primary vehicle for Level 3 behavioral observation.
  • Team huddle guide gives team leads a turnkey system for ongoing reinforcement without requiring additional instructional design support or technology access.
